\begin{abstract}
Learned initial guesses can improve prediction error without reducing accepted numerical correction work. We study this distinction in synthetic one-dimensional elliptic systems, preserving a failed quadratic-ensemble predecessor and evaluating a separately frozen source-linear successor. The original strict-tolerance confirmatory iteration reduction is 0.00\% on IID cases. In the successor's predeclared 129-point, $10^{-6}$-tolerance IID cell, five independent training/evaluation blocks yield 27.73\% mean paired iteration reduction against zero-initialized Laplacian PCG and 21.17\% against a physics-only initial field. The corresponding descriptive block-bootstrap intervals are [27.28, 28.22]\% and [20.94, 21.50]\%. Complete online time improves by 21.72\% and 26.07\%, respectively. The frozen primary rule is satisfied. However, direct tridiagonal solving remains 5.61 times faster by the ratio of mean times. The full successor matrix contains 17280 condition-level rows, with 0 acceptance failures. These findings support only the stated iterative-solver comparison; they do not establish a new solver family, multidimensional superiority, or independent investigator reproduction.
\end{abstract}
