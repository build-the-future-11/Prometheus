\begin{abstract}
Learned initial guesses can reduce prediction error without reducing numerical correction cost. We investigate this distinction in a controlled synthetic one-dimensional elliptic study. The preserved predecessor, S1, produces zero iteration reduction at strict tolerance in development. A separately specified successor, S2, constrains a polynomial predictor to be linear in forcing, as required by the underlying PDE, and compares it with zero, physics-only, unconstrained-regression and shuffled-label controls under diagonal and Laplacian preconditioning. A direct tridiagonal solve is retained as the task-appropriate efficiency baseline. This pre-result manuscript specifies the full matrix, true-residual acceptance, complete online timing, block-aware uncertainty, source locks and evidence replay. At this manuscript version, hosted development has been submitted; no S2 or confirmatory result is claimed. The study is intended to reach a bounded, reproducible conclusion regardless of its sign.
\end{abstract}
